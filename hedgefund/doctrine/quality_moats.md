# Quality & moats doctrine — separating bargains from traps

## The stance
Time is the friend of the wonderful business and the enemy of the mediocre.
A cheap price buys you time to be right; a durable business makes time work
for you. Half of statistically cheap stocks are cheap because they are
dying. Your job: which half.

## Moat taxonomy (require evidence, not vibes)
- **Switching costs**: does losing this supplier hurt the customer's own
  operations? Look for multi-year contracts, integration depth, retention.
- **Network effects**: does each user make the product better for others?
  Rare; claimed 10x more often than it exists.
- **Cost advantage**: structural (scale, location, process) vs temporary
  (input prices). Structural shows up as a DURABLE gross-margin gap vs peers.
- **Intangibles**: brands that confer pricing power (can they raise prices
  above inflation without volume loss?), licenses, patents with years left.
- No moat is fine at the right price — but then the thesis must be assets or
  cash generation, never "recovery to former glory."

## The numbers that reveal quality
- Incremental ROIC: change in operating profit / change in invested capital
  over 3–5 years. This is what compounding is made of. A business earning
  20% on new capital is a machine; one earning 4% is a value trap with
  good marketing.
- Gross margin STABILITY through recessions beats gross margin level.
- Market share trajectory over a decade, not a quarter.

## Value-trap signatures (any two = presume trap)
- Revenue declining for 3+ years while the multiple keeps looking cheaper.
- Managed decline denial: management reinvesting heavily into the dying
  core instead of harvesting cash.
- Technology substitution with no second act (ask: who is eating this
  business, and are they slowing down?).
- Perpetual "transformation" narratives; strategy changes every 2 years.
- High and rising customer concentration.

## The 10-year test
Write one paragraph: what does this company look like in 10 years, and why
does the world still need it? If the honest answer is "smaller and worse,"
the only acceptable thesis is liquidation value with a catalyst — and the
verdict cap is neutral.
