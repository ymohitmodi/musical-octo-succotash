# Valuation doctrine

## The stance
You are buying fractional ownership of a business, not a ticker. Mr. Market
quotes you prices daily; he is your servant, not your guide. Price is what
you pay, value is what you get — your entire job is estimating the gap.

## Owner earnings, not reported earnings
- Value the cash an owner could extract: operating cash flow minus the capex
  required to MAINTAIN the business (not grow it). When maintenance capex is
  unknowable, use full capex — conservatism is free.
- Normalize: use mid-cycle margins, not peak. A cyclical at 8x peak earnings
  is often 20x mid-cycle. The most expensive words in cyclicals: "this time
  the margins hold."
- Adjust for what management hides: stock comp is compensation, "one-time"
  charges that recur annually are operating costs, pension gaps are debt.

## The margin of safety is the strategy
- Demand ≥30% below your conservative intrinsic estimate. The margin is not
  extra return — it is the budget for being wrong, and you will often be wrong.
- The margin must survive the bear case, not the base case. If safety exists
  only under your assumptions, it does not exist.
- Prefer safety from ASSETS (net cash, working capital, hard book value) over
  safety from FORECASTS. A balance sheet is a fact; a projection is a wish.

## Triangulate, never single-model
- Cross-check at least two of: EV/EBIT vs history and peers, FCF yield vs
  bond yields, net asset value, earnings power value (no-growth value).
- If the thesis requires the multiple to EXPAND to work, reject: you are
  betting on sentiment, not value.
- Growth is only worth paying for when incremental returns on capital exceed
  the cost of capital; growth that consumes capital at poor returns DESTROYS
  value while making the income statement look busy.

## Classic errors this fund must not repeat
- Anchoring on the price you first saw, or on the 52-week high.
- Treating a statistically cheap multiple as a thesis. Cheapness is a
  starting flag, never a conclusion — half of cheap is broken.
- DCF theater: 10 precise inputs producing one false certainty. Prefer
  crude conservatism to precise optimism.
- Ignoring dilution: value per SHARE is what you own; a cheap company that
  prints shares is a treadmill.
