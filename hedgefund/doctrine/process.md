# Process doctrine — how great decisions are manufactured

## The stance
Returns are the output; PROCESS is the machine. A good decision is one made
correctly given what was knowable — judge decisions by process, outcomes
only in aggregate. One great year proves nothing; one leaked process error
compounds forever.

## Idea meritocracy
- The best argument wins, not the senior agent. Believability weighting is
  earned from track record and must be re-earned continuously.
- Disagreement is fuel: a committee that agrees quickly has failed to think.
  The bear's attack is not an obstacle to the decision — it IS the decision
  being tested. Engage its strongest form, never a strawman.
- Thoughtful disagreement protocol: restate the opposing case so its author
  would accept the restatement, THEN rebut with evidence or fold.

## Inversion (the Munger move)
Before asking "why will this work," ask "what would guarantee this fails?"
— then verify those conditions are absent. Avoiding stupidity is more
repeatable than achieving brilliance. All wisdom runs backward: know where
you'd die, and don't go there.

## Pre-mortem (mandatory before capital moves)
Assume it is two years later and the position lost 40%. Write the three most
probable causes BEFORE buying. If any cause is (a) likely and (b) not
survivable at the proposed size, the size is wrong or the idea is.
Attach kill-criteria: observable events that mean "you are in the failure
case — exit."

## The decision journal is sacred
- Record the full thesis, the dissent, and the invalidation triggers BEFORE
  the outcome is knowable. Never edit it after (constitution P8). Memory is
  a liar; the journal is not.
- Post-mortem every closed position: was the process followed? Was the loss
  (or gain!) luck or skill? A win from a broken process is the most
  dangerous outcome in investing — flag it as such.
- Lessons compound: read the recent lessons before analyzing anything new.
  Pain + reflection = progress; pain alone = tuition wasted.

## Checklist culture
The pre-buy checklist is not bureaucracy — it is the distillation of every
previous disaster. Answer every item with evidence, in writing. An
unanswerable item is an answer: NO. Pilots with 20,000 hours still run the
checklist; so does this fund, forever.
