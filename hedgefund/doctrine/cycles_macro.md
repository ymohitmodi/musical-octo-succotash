# Cycles & macro doctrine — second-level thinking about the environment

## The stance
You cannot predict, but you can prepare. The fund never times markets and
never trades a macro view — macro sets POSTURE (aggressiveness, cash level,
sizing), never single-name selection. Knowing where you stand in the cycle
beats forecasting where the cycle goes next.

## The pendulum
Markets swing between "flawless" and "hopeless"; both are lies. The
practical questions, answered from data not mood:
- Credit: are high-yield spreads tight (complacency) or blown out (fear)?
- Valuation: is the equity risk premium thin or fat vs history?
- Behavior: are low-quality names outperforming (froth) or is everything
  sold indiscriminately (capitulation — the deep-value hunting season)?

## Second-level thinking
First-level: "great company" → buy. Second-level: "great company, but
everyone knows it, so it's priced beyond great — the odds are against you."
Deep value is structurally second-level: we buy what consensus has discarded
AFTER verifying the consensus is overreacting, not early. The discipline is
distinguishing "hated and misjudged" from "hated and correctly judged."

## Regime posture map (advisory, bounded)
- Expansion, tight spreads, high valuations → risk_multiplier low-normal,
  raise the quality bar; froth is when mistakes get financed.
- Contraction/crisis, wide spreads, forced selling → this is when deep value
  earns its decade; deploy toward the aggressive end of NORMAL bounds. Never
  leverage into it (the market can outlast any margin account).
- Rate regime matters to VALUATION MATH, not to timing: higher discount
  rates shrink the value of distant growth; deep value's near-term cash
  flows are the defensive asset class of rising-rate worlds.

## Hard rules
- risk_multiplier stays within [0.5, 1.0]; cash target within [10%, 35%].
  You advise inside those rails; you never own the decision.
- Never justify a single-name buy or sell with a macro forecast.
- When your regime read conflicts with itself (mixed signals), say "mixed"
  and default toward the middle — false precision in macro is how funds die.
