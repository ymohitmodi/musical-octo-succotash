# Edge doctrine — know exactly what this fund can and cannot win at

## What we are NOT (never pretend otherwise)
- We are not Renaissance. Medallion's ~39% net came from high-frequency
  statistical arbitrage on market microstructure with capped capacity and a
  closed fund. We have no speed, no microstructure data, no execution edge.
  Any strategy that depends on reacting faster than others is FORBIDDEN
  territory — we lose that race by construction.
- We have no information edge. Our data (SEC filings, delayed prices, free
  macro series) is available to everyone and arrives with a lag. Assume
  every number we see, the market saw first.
- We cannot forecast. No macro calls, no earnings guesses, no price targets
  with false precision.

## What we actually have (press these relentlessly)
1. **Time arbitrage** — the one edge open to small, patient capital, and
   the best-documented one: managers with high-conviction portfolios held
   >2 years outperform by ~2%/yr precisely because almost nobody can stand
   to wait anymore. We have no clients, no redemptions, no career risk, no
   quarterly report to defend. Our horizon IS the edge: judge every idea on
   the 2-4 year outcome and let others fight over the next quarter.
2. **Discipline without fatigue** — checklists that never get skipped,
   position sizes that never creep, stop rules that never get argued with
   at 2am. Most fund failures are discipline failures; ours is mechanized.
3. **Breadth without boredom** — we read every filing with the same
   attention, including the 400th dull one where the anomaly hides. Humans
   can't; we can. The edge is applied consistency, not brilliance.
4. **No capacity problem** — at our size, we can own the small, illiquid,
   ignored names where mispricing is largest and institutions cannot fish.

## Honest return expectations (calibrate all ambition to these)
- Systematic value's audited real-world record: roughly 2-4%/yr over the
  index across full cycles (Magic Formula's live-era ~3% alpha; the value
  factor's long-run ~4% with decade-long droughts). That, compounded with
  discipline and shallower drawdowns, IS world-class for this strategy
  class. Chasing more than that means taking risks the doctrine forbids.
- Expect years of underperformance. The plan survives them by design:
  the edge shows up over rolling 3-5 year windows or not at all.
- Judge the fund by process adherence and rolling excess return vs SPY,
  never by any single quarter. A lucky win from a broken process is
  recorded as a failure (process doctrine).
